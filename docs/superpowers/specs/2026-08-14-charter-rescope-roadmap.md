# CHARTER — what survived, what other work already built, and the third still worth shipping

**Status:** roadmap, 2026-08-14. Successor in form to `2026-08-14-neo4j-agent-memory-roadmap.md`, whose twelve steps have now closed. Re-scopes `2026-08-08-charter-expertise-org-design.md`, whose structure half shipped as `tesserae/charter.py` in #120 and whose artifact half this document retires.

## Verdict

**Build about a third of CHARTER, and it is the cheap third. Retire the rest of the spec rather than carry it.**

The prior question first, because two roadmaps have now shipped on top of a document that was never implemented: **is CHARTER still worth building at all?** Yes — but for one reason, not the seven the spec gives, and the reason is measurable.

**The motivating problem reproduces at HEAD.** `_mcp_graph_map` with no scope (`mcp_server.py:3763-3775`) builds a `community_card` for every key of `hierarchy.coarsest`, sorted `(-len(members), cid)`. Measured this session against this project's own `hierarchy.json`: **1,852 scopes, median size 2.0, 1,124 of them with ≤2 members, 83 with ≥100.** The canonical entry point for every agent is a budget-paginated wall whose first discriminator is a member count and whose second is a hash — spec:73-77, still true. Twenty-four shipped steps built views, fusion, tombstones, a vector cache, a transaction clock, a candidate ledger and a sidecar registry. Not one of them was pointed at the entry point.

Against that, `build_charter` run on the same 47,132-node graph this session returns **780 domains, 46,928 member-index entries, 88 routers, 692 leaves, 0 stalls, tiers `{1:7, 2:340, 3:428, 4:5}`, in 11.2s**, byte-identical across two runs, with CH-01 holding exactly (every member held once, zero duplicated, zero missing). Its seven roots are choosable by name: `3d-reconstruction` (8,920), `domain-bfd88123` (7,955), `intake` (7,581), `computer-vision-and-pattern-recognition` (6,637), `python` (6,283), `3d-gaussian-splatting-3` (6,099), `tesserae` (3,453).

**And exactly one property in the spec is unprovided anywhere else: stable identity across ingest.** `community_id` (`community_summaries.py:213`) is `sha256` over sorted member ids — deliberately, so `graph.json` stays byte-idempotent — with the consequence that the key moves whenever membership moves, and the spec's own measurement is that one 15-node document moves ~29% of members. Every cached community summary is therefore invalidated by every ingest. A charter slug does not move: `assign_anchors` measures 97.0% fine / 81.0% coarse anchor preservation against member-set Jaccard failing on ~72% of large scopes, and a no-op reorg on an unchanged graph gives 780 stable / 0 founded / 0 retired. That single property is the module's whole justification, and it is real.

**The defect is not that CHARTER was under-built. It is that the built half is never called.** `build_charter` (`charter.py:482`), `write_charter` (:470) and `succeed` (:667) have **zero production callers**; the only import of the module anywhere in the package is `cli.py:5570`, and it imports `read_charter`. `.tesserae/charter/` does not exist on this repo. 886 lines and 52 passing tests describe an institution nothing derives.

**The half that was never written should stay unwritten.** `render_brief`, the altitude quota/floor table, `altitude_lift`, CH-02 through CH-05 and the behaviour-preserving extraction of `_distill_scope`/`_rollup` out of `agent_distill.py` together constitute a second summarization pipeline. Tesserae already ships the first: `materialize_community_summary` (`community_summaries.py:474-511`) takes an arbitrary `(members, cid, member_ids, level)`, makes exactly one `complete_json` call on a cold cache, caches under `level_cache_path(cache_dir, level, cid)` (:242-250), invalidates on `_members_digest`, rejects prose citing none of its children through `_cites_child_communities` (:331, enforced :418), and **never raises** — every failure returns `None` and the caller keeps its structural card. That is the spec's cost posture (spec:302-306), its citation posture (spec:313-315) and its lazy level-keyed cache (spec:146-150), already shipped. It is missing a stable cid. A slug is a stable cid. Step 3 is four arguments, not 400 lines.

**What the retirement costs, stated plainly, because it is not free.** Of the five CHARTER surfaces the twelve cognitive-memory steps leaned on, this roadmap builds three (`charter_route`, `_domain_clock`, the fallback lint), one already shipped from the other side (`scope=`/`strategy=` on `compile_context`), and two are retired: `render_brief`/altitude quotas and CH-04. Retiring them obliges edits, not silence — `2026-08-08-cognitive-memory-roadmap.md` steps 6, 7, 8 and 9 reason about machinery that will not exist, and the 51-line CH-04 decision record at `charter.py:30-80` is a design commitment about unwritten code that should shrink to a note saying the code was not written and why. Leaving those in place is how a spec gets cited as evidence for six more months.

**Numbers to re-derive before budgeting on them.** The spec's measurements have drifted: 4 balanced divisions → 6 plus intake; intake 5,643 (12.0%) → 7,581 (16.2%); 888 domains → 780; 9 stalls → 0; depth max 5 → 4; build 2.4s → 11.2s; "~900 charter rows" (spec:250) → 780. The ratios hold and the conclusions survive; the absolute figures do not. Two citation errors are worth correcting in place while the spec is open: `partition_graph` is `wiki_projector.py:514`, not `:539` (spec:63), and spec:266-267 cites `project.py:1209-1245` for "the incremental compile already computes touched ids", which is a trends/`code_graph_report` block at HEAD — the fast-clock dirty-set claim rests on a reference that no longer points at anything.

## What already exists

- **Lazy, never-blocking, level-keyed cached abstraction over arbitrary member sets.** `materialize_community_summary` + `level_cache_path` + `community_card` (`hierarchy.py:241-262`), with a warm-cache lookup, an LLM-quality flag and a deterministic structural floor. 52 summaries cached under `.tesserae/community_summaries/`.

- **A sidecar registry, a merge ledger, a persisted vector cache and a lint surface with a fail-loud probe idiom.** `sidecars.py`, `merge_ledger`, `node_vectors` in `graph_stores/sqlite.py:950`, and `lint.py`'s `LINT_PROBE_FAILED` (:1409) / `PENDING_REVIEW` (:1438) pair — a probe that cannot run says so rather than reporting zero.

## Roadmap (ordered by leverage)


### 1. Derive the charter at compile — and fix the anchor selector first, because a slug is permanent
**Cost:** medium · **Borrowed from:** Original. Nothing is borrowed because nothing needs to be written: the code exists and has no caller

**Why now:** Everything else in this roadmap is downstream of one call. `build_charter` (`charter.py:482`), `write_charter` (:470) and `succeed` (:667) have zero production callers; the only package import of the module is `cli.py:5570` for `read_charter`; `.tesserae/charter/` does not exist. Verified this session on the live graph: the call returns 780 domains, 46,928 member-index entries, 88 routers, 692 leaves, 0 stalls, tiers `{1:7, 2:340, 3:428, 4:5}`, in 11.2s, byte-identical across two runs, CH-01 exact.

**Blocking precondition (~6 lines, same PR, not a follow-up):** the slug this step writes is an operator-pinned attach path, and changing it afterwards is a refound. Two of the six divisions are currently unusable — `domain-bfd88123` from the `한 줄 요약` SourceDocument anchor, and `python` from the TechnicalTerm anchor, precisely the case CH-04's own carried-forward caveat names (spec:326-328). The four readable ones all come from ResearchTopic / ResearchField / Paper / Project anchors. The selector at `charter.py:342-347` sorts `ranked` on `(row[0], row[2])` — degree, then id. Adding node type as a middle key, with producer-owned knowledge types ahead of SourceDocument and TechnicalTerm, is a pure change to a pure function and lands before the first byte is written. Fix the selector, never the namer.

**Change:** Call `build_charter` from the `_write_hierarchy_sidecar` call site (`project.py:1566`) — the same graph at the same moment, for the same stated reason: it is the one point where a canonicalized research-layer partition exists and Louvain has not yet seen a `COMMUNITY_SUMMARY` node. With a prior charter on disk, run `succeed(prior, fresh)` and write the result; `--refound` is the only way to discard one. Below the bound the spec's own rule stands (spec:200-204) — fewer than 2 sections, or a research layer under `ARTIFACT_CHAR_BUDGET`, writes nothing and `TESSERAE.md` stays byte-identical.

The ordering window `_write_hierarchy_sidecar` documents at `project.py:2029-2041` applies verbatim to `member_index`: `compile()` rebinds `graph` through `_merge_community_summaries`, `_merge_distillation` and `_write_artifacts` afterwards, so a node named in the charter can be absent from `graph.json` by the time it is written — the same class as the measured 169/1360 sidecar skew. Do not try to close it here; make it visible in step 7.

**Owed in the same PR — three statements that become true or must be deleted:** correct `sidecars.py:211-212` (the charter is derived, not authored; the real reason it is `safe_to_delete=False` is the one `read_charter`'s docstring already gives at :441-446 — deleting it refounds every slug and breaks every pinned attach path); correct `cli.py:5586-5590` so it stops advising a recompile that could never have helped; delete the charter clause at `retrieval/views.py:33-35`, which defends against a hazard that cannot occur. A comment that protects an impossible case is a comment someone will later defend.

**Composes with:** Steps 2 through 7 are all readers of the file this step writes. There is no useful ordering in which it is not first.


### 2. The entry point stops being a size rank
**Cost:** medium · **Borrowed from:** Original — the spec's derivation (spec:70-79) pointed at the surface it named but never wired (spec:349-350)

**Why now:** This is the measurement the whole design exists for, and it reproduces. `_mcp_graph_map` at `scope=None` (`mcp_server.py:3763-3775`) builds a card for every key of `hierarchy.coarsest`, sorted `(-len(members), cid)`: 1,852 cards, median 2 members, 1,124 at ≤2, only 83 at ≥100. An agent picks by rank and page number. The seven roots from step 1 are picked by name. Nothing shipped since 2026-08-08 changed this, and nothing else on any roadmap is aimed at it.

**Change:** `_mcp_graph_map` — when a charter exists, `scope=None` returns division cards instead of the coarsest level, with the dendrogram still reachable at an explicit scope so nothing is removed. `domain:<slug>` becomes a scope in both resolvers: the `find_scope` branch at `mcp_server.py:3786-3796` and `compile_context`'s at `context_compiler.py:616-625`. The second is nearly free — `restrict = set(found_scope[1])` already takes an arbitrary id set, so a domain resolves to its `direct_member_ids` plus its subtree's, and every downstream path is untouched. Both resolvers hardcode "valid scopes are community ids" into their `ValueError`; both messages gain the new grammar in the same edit or they become the fourth and fifth false statements about the charter.

Do NOT invent a second card shape. `community_card` (`hierarchy.py:241-262`) already renders a `(cid, members)` pair with a warm-summary lookup and a deterministic structural floor. A domain is a `(slug, members)` pair. Feed it one.

**Composes with:** Step 3 is what makes a division card say something. Land step 2 without step 3 and the divisions render at structural quality — which is honest and is exactly what `quality: structural` means, but is not yet the payoff.


### 3. A brief is a community summary keyed on a slug — not a new renderer
**Cost:** small · **Borrowed from:** Tesserae's own `materialize_community_summary`, one axis over. This step is the spec's `render_brief` (spec:144, :334) retired and replaced by the thing already doing its job

**Why now:** This is the narrowing that makes the rest affordable, and it rests on a code fact rather than a preference. `materialize_community_summary` (`community_summaries.py:474-511`) takes `(members, cid=, member_ids=, level=, cache_dir=, json_client=, child_cids=)`, makes exactly one `complete_json` call on a cold cache, caches under `level_cache_path(cache_dir, level, cid)` (:242-250), invalidates on `_members_digest`, rejects prose citing none of its children through `_cites_child_communities` (:331, enforced :418), and **never raises** — every failure path returns `None` and the caller keeps its structural card. Read against the spec: that is settled decision 5 (lazy leaf briefs), the never-blocking `quality: structural` posture (spec:302-306), the `(level, cid)` cache key the spec itself cites as its model (spec:146-150), and the `_cites_child_communities` posture the six lints were introduced by name as following (spec:313-315).

What it lacks is one thing, and that thing is what CHARTER supplies. `community_id` (`community_summaries.py:213`) is `sha256` over sorted member ids — deliberately, so node identity stays stable across content edits and `graph.json` stays byte-idempotent — with the consequence that the key moves whenever membership moves. One 15-node document moves ~29% of members. So every cached summary is invalidated by every ingest, and no amount of caching fixes it. A charter slug does not move.

**Change:** Pass `cid=<slug>` and `level=<tier>` into the existing call. Nothing else. The cache path becomes `.tesserae/community_summaries/<tier>/CommunitySummary_<slug>.json` — a namespace `level_cache_path`'s own docstring already reserves against the compile pass's flat top-level files, so the two writers still cannot collide on a path. `community_card`'s warm lookup then serves domain cards unchanged, and the altitude question answers itself: a domain read one tier up is the same member set at a coarser `level`, which is the cache key the function already takes.

**Correction, as implemented (2026-08-15, found by review of the step-7 PR):** the cache path above is WRONG, and reading it instead of the merged code shipped a structurally dead lint. `cid` is not `<slug>` — `brief_cid` mints `CharterDomain:<slug>`, so the file is `<tier>/CharterDomain_<slug>.json`. The reason is in `_BRIEF_CID_PREFIX`'s own comment: `prune_stale_summary_caches` deletes every `CommunitySummary_*.json` whose name is absent from the hierarchy's live-cid manifest, and a charter slug is never in that manifest, so minting briefs into that namespace would delete every brief on the next compile — the per-ingest cache wipe this step exists to end, arriving through a different door. Step 7's `CHARTER_FALLBACK` restated the path from this paragraph, matched no file the package writes, counted every live domain cold, and could therefore never reach its own frozen-slice branch. Nothing should spell that path out again: `charter.brief_cache_path(charter, slug, cache_dir=…)` composes it from the writer's own pieces.

The cost avoided is the reason to state this as its own step. The spec's alternative is new-code items 1 (`render_brief`), 2 (lifting `_distill_scope` out of `distill_agent` and `_rollup` out of `_distill_manager`, inside a 3,124-line module whose byte-for-byte determinism tests are the only oracle) and 4 (altitude selection, the quota table, `altitude_lift`) — a second summarizer over 692 leaves and 6,735,127 chars of leaf mass, on the order of 719 LLM calls for one full materialization. Lazy amortizes the calls. It does not justify the second pipeline.

**Composes with:** Step 2's cards and step 5's route both read whatever this produces; step 7's census counts what it could not produce. This is also where `## Open` (spec:387-392) closes as moot rather than answered — with no carry quota there is nothing to re-set.


### 4. `## Divisions` replaces twelve alphabetically-sorted nodes
**Cost:** small · **Borrowed from:** Original — the `## Divisions` block of spec new-code item 5 (spec:352), the only part of the spec's attach story with a named mechanism

**Why now:** `render_harness_context` (`agent_harness.py:203-262`) is the first thing every agent reads, and its `## Representative nodes` section is `sorted(graph.nodes, key=node_sort_key)[:12]` with `node_sort_key = (node.type.value, node.name.lower())` (:280-281). Twelve nodes out of 47,132, chosen by type name then alphabetically. That is the current answer to "what is this project about", and the division list step 1 already produces is a strictly better one at zero marginal cost. The harness file on this repo is 2,955 bytes; seven lines fit without pressure on any budget.

**Change:** A `## Divisions` block between `## Graph summary` (:253) and `## Representative nodes` (:258), one line per live tier-1 domain — slug, member count, anchor name — read from `charter.json`. No charter means no block, which is the below-the-bound case (spec:200-204) and must stay byte-identical to today's output.

*Correction, as implemented:* not "tier-1", and not in size order. Step 2 shipped first and defined the root as `charter.live_divisions` — a live domain with **no live parent**, in slug order — so the block calls that function rather than re-deriving the set here. The two entry points an agent reads would otherwise disagree on exactly the axis this milestone exists to fix: keying on `tier` hid an orphan that `graph_map()` listed, and size order opened the file with `intake` (7,581 members, `anchor_id: ""`), a line carrying no name. The count is `domain_members`' live-subtree walk, matching the `size` step 2's domain card reports; it stays on the line as information rather than as the sort key.

Do NOT build `domains attach` here. The spec asserts (spec:245-248) that "the agent's config already contains `.tesserae/agent_harness/domains/<slug>/TESSERAE.md`" and names no mechanism by which that path enters a config. **Composes with:** Step 5 — an agent that reads the division list and still cannot choose is exactly the case `charter_route` exists for.


### 5. `charter_route` — one call over 780 rows, honest about being best-effort
**Cost:** small · **Borrowed from:** Original — spec:249-251, with the honesty split at spec:257-258 already adopted verbatim by cognitive-roadmap step 1

**Why now:** Two shipped steps compose against this tool by name and it does not exist in any form. Cognitive-roadmap step 7 records the gap itself ("`charter_route` does not exist yet") and honours its ordering constraint structurally instead, through the comment at `context_compiler.py:545-550`; step 11 states it "sits directly on charter_route" and its planner catalog contains no charter row. Neither is wrong and neither can be finished. The corpus size is now measured rather than estimated: 780 domains, not the spec's "~900" (spec:250) — and the point of the figure survives the correction, since it is 780 rows against 47,132 nodes.

**Change:** A `charter_route(task, altitude="auto")` MCP tool: `hybrid_search` (`retrieval/hybrid.py`) over a corpus built from each domain's slug, anchor name and the tags its warm summary carries; greedy beam-1 descent through `child_slugs`; ties on `(-score, slug)`; returns `{path, brief, parent, siblings}`. Worst case is 1 + depth, which measures at 5 rather than the spec's 6 now that max tier is 4.

Take the honesty split into the tool description rather than a doc: the charter file is byte-idempotent, the route is best-effort and varies with the embedding backend — the same distinction `active_embedding_backend` already warns about one layer down. The Neo4j roadmap's `node_vectors` cache (`graph_stores/sqlite.py:950`) is what makes embedding a 780-row corpus per route affordable; without it this step re-embeds on every call, which is the deficit that step existed to remove.

**Composes with:** Step 4's division list is the zero-call path and this is the one-call path. Both read the same file, and an agent that has the first rarely needs the second — which is the spec's own claim about attached versus routed mode (spec:245-251), now testable.


### 6. `_domain_clock` dates a domain from its sources, and degrades instead of raising
**Cost:** small · **Borrowed from:** Original — the never-invent-a-timestamp posture (spec:297-300), corrected once by cognitive-roadmap step 3's explicit withdrawal and again by measurement

**Why now:** The spec defines `_domain_clock(members, as_of)` as `max(first_seen_at)`, hard-failing when no member carries one. Measured: `metadata['first_seen_at']` is present on 1,213 of 47,132 nodes — **2.6%** — so a literal implementation raises `DistillError` on **738 of 778 domains**. Cognitive-roadmap step 3 already withdrew the claim that it unblocked this and named the fix: call `temporal._source_ts(node, graph_project_roots(graph))` (`temporal.py:267`, roots at :191), whose ladder is metadata keys, then a leading date in the node's own name, then a dated directory segment of `source_path` relative to a project root. That lifts coverage to 81.5% of nodes and drops the failure to **50 domains (6%)**. `temporal.py:176` already names `_domain_clock` as a required caller of `_source_ts`.

The residue is a decision the spec cannot make on our behalf: 6% of domains have no resolvable timestamp on any rung, and hard-failing denies them a brief permanently — a domain frozen not because it changed but because nothing dated it. Degrade instead: `distilled_through: null` plus `quality: undated`. That keeps never-invent-a-timestamp fully intact — both paths read only bytes already in `graph.json`, no wall clock, no git, so two runs any wall-time apart stay byte-identical — while keeping the never-blocking posture step 3 inherits.

**Change:** `_domain_clock(members, as_of)` in `charter.py`, calling `_source_ts` with `graph_project_roots(graph)` and returning `None` on an exhausted ladder rather than raising. Router clock stays the spec's recursive `max(children.distilled_through)`, with `None` absorbing. Count the nulls in step 7.

**Composes with:** Step 7 reports what this returns `None` for. A rising undated count is an extraction-quality signal of exactly the kind settled decision 7 already accepted for intake, and it should be read the same way.


### 7. Two lints, not six — CH-01 at runtime and the fallback census
**Cost:** small · **Borrowed from:** CH-01 (spec:319) and CH-06 (spec:324). CH-02 through CH-05 are retired below

**Why now:** CH-01 holds today by construction and is asserted in three tests (`tests/test_charter.py:303`, `:390`, `:588`) — re-verified this session on the live graph, 46,928 members held exactly once, zero duplicated, zero missing. What no unit test can cover is the case step 1 creates. `_write_hierarchy_sidecar`'s documented ordering window (`project.py:2029-2041`) means `compile()` rebinds `graph` through three later passes, so a node named in `member_index` can be absent from `graph.json` by the time that file is written — the same systematic cause behind the measured 169/1360 sidecar skew, and not detectable from a fixture. A partition true against the graph handed to `build_charter` and false against the graph on disk is precisely what CH-01 exists to catch.
